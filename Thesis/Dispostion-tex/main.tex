\section*{Introduction}
\cite{janowiczGeoAISpatiallyExplicit2020} asked if there will be an \textit{"artificial GIS analyst"} by 2030. Since then there have been huge strides both in the field of \gls{ai} and autonomous \gls{gis} (\cite{liGIScienceEraArtificial2025}). But using \gls{llms} for spatial analysis requires large amounts of compute, leading to low accessibility and high environmental impact (\cite{janowiczPhilosophicalFoundationsGeoAI2023, shiGeographyAISustainability2025}). \gls{llms} often produce unnecessary reasoning tokens with high latency and a long response in natural language, which is difficult to process downstream (\cite{wangTruncatedReasoningBudget2026,chenNOTThinkThat2025,duanGeneralDecisionModels2026}). \textit{Decision models}, also described as \textit{system-one} models, might be more suitable for certain GeoAI (\cite{janowiczGeoAISpatiallyExplicit2020}) applications, as they operate more efficiently and quickly than \acrshort{llms}, as well as responding in a predetermined, consistent format. To test this assumption, this thesis reproduces the experiment on \textit{spatial relation qualification} done by \cite{jiFoundationModelsGeospatial2025} using Typesafe AI's decision model Jev (\cite{IntroducingSystemOne}).

\section*{Research Goals}
The aim of this thesis is to gain initial insights on decision models in GeoAI by reproducing the experiment on spatial relation qualification in \cite{jiFoundationModelsGeospatial2025}. This will enable a comparison between ChatGPT-4 and Jev on the same task. As Jev is not a chat-based model, the prompt engineering methods used in the original experiment, few-shot learning and chain-of-thought reasoning, might not yield the same benefit. Ethical points, especially sustainability, but also bias (\cite{aldahoulJevDecisionModel2026}), will be discussed too. In total, the thesis links up with works about GeoAI (\cite{jiFoundationModelsGeospatial2025,janowiczGeoAISpatiallyExplicit2020}), autonomous GIS (\cite{liGIScienceEraArtificial2025}), and ethical use of GeoAI (\cite{shiGeographyAISustainability2025, janowiczPhilosophicalFoundationsGeoAI2023}). The thesis also wants to contribute to works evaluating and benchmarking Jev (\cite{wangTruncatedReasoningBudget2026, duanGeneralDecisionModels2026,deusserEvaluatingBenchmarkingSystem2026}).
\newpage

The following research questions will be addressed:
\begin{itemize}
    \item How does the decision model Jev compare with ChatGPT-4 in the task of spatial relation qualification in terms of accuracy and cost?
    \item In what ways could decision models benefit GeoAI, especially considering sustainability and integration in autonomous \gls{gis}?
    \item Does few-shot prompting and chain-of-thought reasoning improve results for system-one intelligence models?
\end{itemize}

\section*{Methodology}
To ensure a good comparison, the thesis will stay as true as possible to the original experiment in \cite{jiFoundationModelsGeospatial2025}, using the same data and setup. The prompts will have to be adapted to the unique requirements of Jev, but the same strategies (few-shot prompting and chain-of-thought reasoning) will be tested, to the extent possible with Jev. The experiment will utilize Typesafe AI's API and Python SDK to access Jev 1.13.0. All code and results will be made available together with the thesis on GitHub. A short review of related works will accompany the experiment along with a discussion on the current state of decision models and their advantages as well as shortcomings for GeoAI.

\section*{Expected Results and Challenges}
Initial experimentation proved Jev's speed and (cost-)efficiency, while it could not beat the benchmark set by ChatGPT-4 in terms of accuracy. Still, the confidence score, which Jev assigns to its answers, seems reliable, and some interesting patterns occurred in the initial results, which merit further investigation.

The main challenge this thesis will face is a problem common with all LLMs: Their opacity. Jev's answers lack any reasoning and cannot be reproduced reliably. In addition, Jev is a proprietary model, with limited official information released publicly.
