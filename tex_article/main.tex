\section*{Introduction}
Since the release of the first chatbot powered by a large language model (LLM) in late 2022, it has become clear that artificial intelligence (AI) has left the realm of science fiction to enter our daily lifes. Since then almost all spheres of society have been impacted in some way by AI, Spatial Data Science being no exception. There have been many works testing LLMs in a variety of spatial applications [EXAMPLES] and some discussing their caveats, challenges, and shortcomings [EXAMPLES]. This thesis sees itself in both categories, recognizing concerns raised over the sustainability of GeoAI \cite{janowiczPhilosophicalFoundationsGeoAI2023,shiGeographyAISustainability2025} and evaluating a new variant of LLM for a spatial task. This thesis tests the spatial understanding of the system-one [cite system one] intelligence model Jev, which was released by the company Typesafe AI in September 2026. To this end, the experiment on spatial relation qualification done by Yuhan Ji (et al, 2025 \cite{jiFoundationModelsGeospatial2025}) is reproduced using Jev instead of ChatGPT-4. Compared to ChatGPT-4 Jev is quicker, cheaper, and outputs in a fixed format, which facilitates downstream processing of the results. The question remains if the accuracy of Jev's answers is comparable to GPT-4, which this thesis will attempt to find out.

\section*{Research Goals}
GeoAI has been tested for many applications in the field of spatial data science [EXAMPLES], one of them being spatial relation qualification \cite{jiFoundationModelsGeospatial2025}. Such exploration helps us understand how LLMs represent and analyse spatial information and how (agentic) AI can aid in geoprocessing tasks (cite GeoAutomatom). At the same time there are ethical concerns with GeoAI \cite{janowiczPhilosophicalFoundationsGeoAI2023}, a major example being sustainability. Geodata is usually high in volume and temporal resolution, thus requiring many processing steps and, especially if LLMs are involved, substantial compute. Typesafe AI's Jev is set up to process high volumes of data efficiently, quickly, and reliably, which makes it seem suitable for GeoAI. 

This thesis will put this assumption to the test, reproducing the experiment of spatial relation qualification made by Yuhan Ji (et al, 2025 \cite{jiFoundationModelsGeospatial2025}) using Jev. This will enable a direct comparison between ChatGPT-4 and Jev, highlighting advantages and disadvantages of both models. Doing so the thesis hopes to answer the following research questions:

\begin{itemize}
    \item How does Jev fare with spatial relation qualification?
    \item In what ways could system-one intelligence models benefit GeoAI, especially considering aspects of sustainability?
    \item Do proven prompt engineering methods like few-shot prompting and Chain-of-Thought reasoning work for system-one intelligence models?
\end{itemize}

\section*{Methodology}
To ensure a good comparison this thesis will stay as true as possible to the original experiment in \cite{jiFoundationModelsGeospatial2025}, using the same data and setup. The prompts will have to be adapted to the unique requirements of Jev. The experiment will utilize Typesafe AI's API and Python SDK to utilize Jev. All code and results will be made available together with the thesis on Github. A short review of related works will accompany the experiment.

\section*{Expected Results and Challenges}
Initial experimentation proved Jev's speed and (cost-)efficiency, while it could not beat the benchmark set by ChatGPT-4 in terms of accuracy. Still, the confidence score, which Jev assigns to its answers, seems reliable, and some interesting patterns occurred in the inital results, which merit further investigation.

The main challenge this thesis will face is a problem common with all LLMs: Their opacity. Jev's answers especially lack any reasoning and cannot be reproduced reliably. In addition, Jev is a proprietary model, with very little official information released publicly.
